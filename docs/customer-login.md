# Client Login (magic-link sign-in)

`/client-login` lets a customer request a one-time sign-in link by phone or email. It is off until `CUSTOMER_LOGIN_ENABLED=true`; while off, `/api/customer-login` and `/api/customer-login-verify` answer 404. On load the page asks `GET /api/customer-login` whether sign-in can work (404 off, 503 not ready, 405 ready) and, when it is off, not ready or unreachable, shows **Text us for a secure link** straight away instead of the form. The site navigation does not link to the page yet (a later site unit adds it once the account portal exists); the customer portal's error screen links to it as a secondary **Get a new sign-in link** action, after its existing **Message the team here** form, which stays the primary action while Client Login is off by default.

## Flow

1. The page posts `{identifier}` to `POST /api/customer-login`. The request must carry the exact site `Origin`, `Content-Type: application/json` and `X-EGC-Portal: 1`, and be at most 2 KB. A honeypot field (`botcheck`) is accepted and ignored. Before any lookup, the request (and the page's probe) answers 503 **Text us** while no link could be sent: `EGC_MESSAGING_ENABLED` is off, the link keys are not configured, or the owner has not approved the `portal_magic_link` wording. That is global configuration, the same for every phone and email, so it reveals nothing about any of them. The wording check is cached in each Functions isolate for one minute (ten seconds after a Firestore read error), so probes and posts do not each read `message_templates`; an owner approval takes effect within a minute, without a deploy.
2. Every well-formed request gets the same `202 {ok:true, message}` answer, padded to at least 700 ms. The lookup and send run afterwards (`waitUntil`), so the answer and its timing never depend on whether the phone or email is on file, ambiguous, on DND or rate limited. Only a malformed phone/email gets a 400, and that says nothing about existence.
3. In the background:
   - Fixed-window limits: 10 requests per 15 minutes per client address (`CF-Connecting-IP`; an IPv6 client is counted by its /64, since it can rotate through the rest of it, and an IPv4-mapped address as its IPv4 address) and 3 per 15 minutes per normalized phone/email. Rolling 24-hour limits stop a slow drip (one request every ten minutes, all night): 5 requests per normalized phone/email, and 3 sign-in texts per customer, counted on the resolved customer just before the send, so a phone and an email for the same customer share it. The customer cap counts texts, not taps: a request that texts nothing gives its slot back (a repeat inside the approved-send ten-minute send key, a send already in flight there, or a refusal before the send such as DND, `notify:false`, no SMS consent or no usable contact), so three quick taps cost one slot. Any other answer, or an error, keeps the slot, and a give-back that cannot be confirmed leaves it counted. Checks run in that order and stop at the first refusal, so a refused request is not counted by the later limits. Counters live in `rate_limits/{HMAC(bucket|key|window)}` (a rolling limit keeps only the times of the attempts still inside its day); the IP, identifier and customer id are never stored. Increments are compare-and-set commits; contention or a malformed counter fails closed. A customer whose number is being abused may not receive a link for up to a day; they can still text us.
   - The customer must be the single exact match from `customer-identity.js` (normalized `phoneE164`/`emailLower`). Ambiguous, unverified or stale matches send nothing, and the saved record must still carry exactly what was typed.
   - The link is sent through the approved-send service as `portal_magic_link` with `customer_initiated` approval: the owner must approve the wording in **Message templates**, HighLevel DND, the no-SMS-consent tag and `notify:false` are respected, and delivery goes only to the saved contact on the customer record (a typed email still texts the saved phone when the template is SMS). The service's ten-minute send key allows at most one link per customer per ten minutes; failed sends are recorded and never retried automatically, and uncertain sends are never repeated.
   - The link (`/api/customer-login-verify?token=…`) is created at send time: a random 256-bit token, stored only as `customer_login_links/{HMAC(token)}` with the customer, the customer's `portalSessionVersion`, `expiresAt` (15 minutes) and `usedAt:null`.
4. `GET /api/customer-login-verify` only renders a confirm page (no storage access), so link scanners and previews never spend the token. It needs exactly one well-formed `token`; anything a click tracker appends (`utm_*` and the like) is ignored and never read or echoed. Its **Sign in to my projects** form posts the token back. The form works without JavaScript; the page's same-origin `client-login.js` only disables the button after the first tap, so a double tap cannot spend the link on a request the browser then abandons.
5. `POST /api/customer-login-verify` (same origin, form-encoded) consumes the token in one commit: the link's `usedAt` compare-and-set, a create-only `customer_sessions/{HMAC(session token)}` record `{customerId, sessionVersion, via:'magic_link', createdAt, expiresAt (30 days), revokedAt:null}` and a fence on the customer record. It sets `__Host-egc_customer` (HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Lax, Path=/, 30 days) and answers 303:
   - to `/customer-portal`, with the existing per-job portal cookie for the customer's soonest upcoming (else most recent) non-cancelled project, bound to that account root's current link version; or
   - to `/client-login?status=signed_in` when there is no project to open yet (any per-job portal cookie left on this browser is then cleared).
   A project linked to a business account (non-empty `businessAccountId` on the job or its account root, the B2B-SAFE rule in `docs/business-client-hub.md`) is never the landing project and never gets a homeowner owner-level portal session this way, whatever its link version: its company members open it from the Business Hub under their roles.
   The landing project and its cookie are worked out before the link is spent. If that lookup (or any read before the commit) fails, the answer is a 503 **Sign-in didn't finish** page whose **Try again** button posts the same link again; the link stays unused and no cookie is set or cleared.
   An expired or revoked link lands on `/client-login?status=expired`. A link that was already used lands on `/client-login?status=used`, which says so and offers **Open my projects** (`/customer-portal`): after a double tap, the first request may already have signed this browser in. A new link for the same customer can only be sent once the approved-send ten-minute window has passed, and at most three are sent per customer in any 24 hours, so the page says to wait ten minutes and, if no link arrives, to text us.

## Logging

Every accepted request writes one line to the Pages Functions log from its background work, for example `{"event":"customer_login","outcome":"send","status":"dry_run"}`, and every **Text us** 503 (a POST or the page's probe) writes one with its reason, for example `{"event":"customer_login","outcome":"unavailable","reason":"template_unapproved"}`. The 404 while the flag is off logs nothing. Each line carries only internal codes (`outcome`, `status`, `reason`, `code`, `bucket`); any value that is not already a plain code is logged as `redacted`. A line never contains the phone, email, IP address, customer id, link or token. The `unavailable` line is written once per reason per minute in each isolate, so a looping client cannot flood the log. Outcomes: `unavailable` (with the `reason`: `messaging_disabled`, `link_keys_unavailable`, `template_unapproved` or `template_unreadable`), `send` (with the approved-send `status`, such as `submitted`, `already_sent`, `dry_run`, `suppressed`, `failed` or `uncertain`, and its `reason`), `unknown`, `ambiguous`, `unverified`, `contact_mismatch`, `account_review`, `rate_limited` (with the `bucket`: `customer_login_ip`, `customer_login_identifier`, `customer_login_identifier_day` or `customer_login_customer_day`), `honeypot` and `error` (with the error `code`). When customers say no link arrived, these lines show why.

## Account reads

The account-level project list (`readCustomerAccountContext`) and the account session reader (`readCustomerAccountSession`) are ready for the account portal unit. Every read re-queries the customer's jobs, skips private rows and re-verifies each job's account root, so a job moved to another customer disappears on the next request, and a job id supplied by the browser answers the same 404 whether it is missing or belongs to someone else. The jobs query is masked to the fields and named map subfields the DTOs read (never whole quotes, line items, approval signatures or payment maps), and pages on by document id, so a legacy job id with other characters is a valid cursor even though that row is never shown. Quotes follow the business hub's release rule (`projectReleased`): a draft, void, withdrawn, superseded, not-ready, not-issued or never-sent quote is not shown. Business-linked projects (job or account root) are left out of every list (jobs, quotes, invoices, receipts) and only counted in `coverage.businessProjects`; asking for one by id answers 409 `CUSTOMER_ACCOUNT_JOB_BUSINESS`, so the account portal can never hand one an owner session.

## Revoking sessions

A session is valid only while `customers/{id}.portalSessionVersion` (absent = 0) equals the version it was created with. Owners and managers sign a customer out of every device with `POST /api/customer-account-revoke {requestId, customerId, expectedRevision}`. One commit bumps `portalSessionVersion` (every account session and unused sign-in link) and the `customerPortalLinkVersion` of each account root of the customer's projects, closed and business-linked ones included (the per-job owner cookie handed out at sign-in, homeowner portal links staff shared, and collaborator links bound to that version), each root conditioned on the revision that was read, with an idempotency receipt and a `hub_audit` entry for the customer and for each root. Each project's root is resolved exactly as the portal resolves it when a link is opened (`customerAccountOwnerJobId`, else the project itself), so a legacy project with no type is covered too. A root with an unreadable link version is left for an owner to review (the portal already refuses its links), and a project whose owner chain needs review is counted in `needsReview`, which the answer returns. `portalLinksComplete` is true only when every project was read, `needsReview` is 0 and every root was bumped; it is false when the customer has more than 200 projects. Company (`biz_`) sessions follow the business account and are not affected, and the saved authorized people are kept (use `/api/customer-portal-revoke` to clear them). If a project changes while the revocation is being written, nothing is applied and the request answers 409.

There is no customer-facing sign-out route yet: `signOutCustomerAccountSession` (marks this browser's session revoked and clears `__Host-egc_customer`) is ready for the account portal unit, which adds the same-origin sign-out POST and its button.

## Storage and security

- `rate_limits`, `customer_login_links` and `customer_sessions` are server-only (explicit deny rules). Rolling rate-limit records carry `expiresAt` one day after their latest attempt, so the same cleanup removes them. They hold keyed digests only; raw link and session tokens exist only in the SMS/email and the browser cookie.
- Digests use the purpose keys `egc/rate-limit/v1`, `egc/magic-link/v1` and `egc/customer-account-session/v1` (from `HUB_PURPOSE_KEY_SECRET`, else `HUB_SESSION_SECRET`). Changing that root invalidates outstanding links and sessions.
- Expired records are deleted opportunistically (about one request in sixteen, at most 50 per collection, one hour after expiry). A Firestore TTL policy on `expiresAt` is optional.
- `/client-login*` and the verify page are `noindex`, `no-store`, unframeable and served under a strict CSP (`script-src 'self'`, no inline script or style). The verify page uses `Referrer-Policy: strict-origin`: under `no-referrer` a browser form POST sends `Origin: null`, and `strict-origin` still never sends the token-bearing URL.

## Owner checklist

1. Run the customer identity backfill (`docs/customer-identity-backfill.md`) so saved customers carry `phoneE164`/`emailLower`; customers without them cannot receive a link.
2. Approve the **Portal sign-in link** (`portal_magic_link`) wording in Message templates.
3. On Preview first: set `CUSTOMER_LOGIN_ENABLED=true` with `EGC_MESSAGING_ENABLED=true` and `EGC_MESSAGING_DRY_RUN` unset (dry run), confirm the `message_sends` ledger records `dry_run` and the Functions log shows `"outcome":"send","status":"dry_run"`, then set `EGC_MESSAGING_DRY_RUN=false` and sign in with an owner-controlled test customer. Until the wording is approved the page shows **Text us for a secure link**.
4. Deploy the Firestore rules from this repository.
5. Only then enable it in Production.
