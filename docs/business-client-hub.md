# B2B business client hub

## Routes and operations

- Client: `/business-hub`. This is a company workspace, separate from `/customer-portal` (one shared project) and `/book` (walkthrough request).
- Staff: `/business-hub?staff=1`. Existing authorized EGC staff sign-in is required. Business managers can manage all companies and explicitly link projects. Staff with the verified `sales` role can onboard and service only companies they created. No role is granted by recognizing a name or email in client-side code.
- Primary contact: Zoe Zoll, zoe.zoll@easygaragecleaning.com, +1 970 999 1403.
- Backend: `functions/api/business-hub.js`, the existing server-side Firebase service-account configuration, and default-deny Firestore collections. No additional SaaS or public Firebase permission is required.

## First account

1. Authorized staff signs in, chooses **Create business account**, and records company, named client administrator and billing email.
2. The server creates the company and single-use 48-hour invitation atomically with an audit entry. Either copy the link and deliver it privately to the named person, or, when invitation email is on, choose **Email it from EGC** and confirm. **No invitation email is ever sent without that explicit staff choice.** See "Invitation delivery" below.
3. The client redeems the link and receives a seven-day HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Strict session. Add properties, contacts/access notes, work requests and team members. Avoid permanent access codes in saved notes.
4. Review requests in the staff workspace. A requested date is not a confirmed appointment. Scheduling still uses EGC dispatch and existing crew-capacity checks.
5. An EGC business manager selects a property and enters an existing canonical job ID. Explicitly confirm that the company is authorized to see the project AND billing. A referral alone is not authorization. Linking updates only B2B sharing fields; it does not change attribution, quotes, payments or appointment state.
6. Sent/accepted quotes and issued invoices appear from the canonical job. The client opens an authorized project to use the existing approval, payment, messaging and photo tools. Draft quotes and internal notes are not published by this hub. An issued invoice shows its effective status at the time of the request (money-core `invoiceStatus`: Overdue from the first Denver day after its due date, Paid, Partially paid, Payment pending verification or Issued), never the status saved when it was issued; nothing owed reads Paid once the money is verified (a verified payment, or with no payment record a verified deposit that covers it, the same rule as the payment review flag), else Payment pending verification; while a recorded payment awaits review, a partly paid invoice reads Payment pending verification. Due dates read like `Oct 15, 2026` (the Denver calendar day, on any device time zone); a saved date that is not a real calendar day is not shown, as money-core never treats it as a due date. Members who cannot pay (managers and viewers) get **View**; administrators and billing users get **View / pay**.

## Roles

Company administrator: properties, requests, approvals, payments and team invitations/revocation. Property manager: properties, requests and approvals, not payments or team administration. Billing: account view and payments, not requests/approvals/team administration. Viewer: read-only. Members share the company's authorized portfolio unless their access is limited to selected properties (see **Per-property access** below). Prices are never accepted from a browser.

Inviting an existing member (by email) renews them: it issues a new single-use link, bumps their generation and invalidates any prior session. Renewal works for pending, expired and revoked invitations and for active members whose seven-day sign-in has ended, whether it expired or they signed out. A member who is still signed in keeps their session, and a renewal attempt returns 409 without changing them. Revoke first only if they need a replacement link now. Each redeemed link creates exactly one session, and the member records when it ends (`sessionExpiresAt`, set on redeem and cleared on sign-out as a best effort). An active member saved before that field existed is treated as renewable, which is the earlier behaviour. The field only decides renewal and is never used for sign-in; the session record stays authoritative. Administrators cannot renew their own link while signed in. There is no email/password self-service recovery in this release. Revoke a member or unlink a project to end delegated project access on the next request. Customers cannot create their own company or claim access based on a domain, guessed job ID or shared phone number.

Retries are safe. `create_account`, `invite_member`, `resend_invite`, `reset_sign_in` and a new `save_property` accept a client `requestId` (32 hex characters); `resend_invite` and `reset_sign_in` require it, and so does any email delivery. For the invitation actions the receipt lives in `business_operations/{requestId}`, and a replay returns `{duplicate:true}` with the saved delivery status while that link is still the member's current access, otherwise `superseded` (a newer link) or `revoked`. It never returns a token and never sends again. Retrying with the same id and the same fields returns `{duplicate:true}` and the original account or property id. A different payload returns 409. A retried onboarding never returns a second invitation token; staff reissue the link through the team invite. `request_service` and `message` were already idempotent by `requestId`/`messageId`. Requests without an id keep the earlier behaviour.

Client views show member ids and emails only to people with team permission (administrators). They need the ids for Revoke, and the ids already appear in the invite links they create. An id alone grants no access. Other roles see names, roles and statuses only. Clients never see staff usernames or internal author ids. This is a deliberate departure from hiding all member ids. If ids must be hidden from administrators too, change `revoke_member` to take the member's email.

## Per-property access

An administrator (or EGC staff) can limit a property manager, billing user or viewer to selected properties, either when inviting them (property checkboxes on **Invite a colleague**) or later with **Limit to properties** / **Edit property access** on **Team access**. The team table shows **All properties** or an **Access limited to N properties** badge, and a limited member sees the same badge on every tab.

- Stored as an optional `propertyIds` list on the member. A member without it (every member saved before this release) or with an empty list sees every property, so no backfill is needed. Administrators always see every property: an administrator cannot be limited, and renewing a limited member as an administrator clears the list. So the last administrator always keeps full access.
- A limited member sees only their properties, the requests and linked projects of those properties, and messages tied to those requests. General account messages stay visible. A request shows its project's job id only while that project is actively linked to one of their properties, so a project that staff re-link to another property is not named on the old request. Project totals, invoices, balances and the invoice CSV come from the same filtered snapshot, and server exports (`?export=`) and snapshot decorators receive the filtered account and jobs.
- Enforced on the server on every request, not only hidden in the page: `request_service`, `message` with a `requestId`, `open_project` and editing a property answer 403 for another property even with direct ids. Only members with access to every property (and EGC staff) can add a property, because a new property would be outside every limited list. Delegated `/customer-portal` project access is rechecked on each request (`readBusinessProjectViewer`), so narrowing access denies an already-open project on its next request.
- The limit is read live from the saved member, so changing it does not bump the member's generation and does not sign them out. Revoke the member if they must lose all access at once.
- API: `set_member_properties {memberId, propertyIds, requestId?}` needs team permission (client administrators and EGC staff). `propertyIds` is `null` or `[]` for every property, or ids of this account's properties (at most 100). Unknown ids, ids of another account, prototype keys and malformed values return 400; an administrator target or a revoked member returns 409. Repeating the current setting returns `{ok:true, unchanged:true}` without changing the member or adding audit rows. A `requestId` still records its receipt, so the same id can never later apply a different change and its replay still answers `{duplicate:true}` (the current state alone cannot tell a replay from a new request); like every receipt it expires (see **Retention**). `invite_member` accepts the same `propertyIds`; when it is omitted, a renewed member keeps their saved list and a new member gets every property. On **Invite a colleague**, typing the email of an existing member starts the property choice from their saved access, unless the administrator already changed the choice; a note says which applies.
- Audit: `invite_member`, `revoke_member` and `set_member_properties` add a `hub_audit` entry (via `b2b`, actions `business.member_invited`, `business.member_revoked`, `business.member_properties`) with before/after member summaries (name, email, role, status, generation and `properties: 'all' | [ids]`) in the same commit as the change. The `business_audit` row for those actions and for `save_property` also records `requestId` and the before/after member or property summary (property summaries hold only id, name and address, never access notes). A 32-hex `requestId` that is a UUID without dashes (as the page generates) is stored on the `hub_audit` entry in UUID form.
- Retries: `set_member_properties`, `invite_member` and `revoke_member` with a `requestId` write a create-only receipt to `business_operations` in the same commit as the change. Repeating the request (same account, actor, action, member and fields; property order does not matter) returns `{ok:true, duplicate:true}` and applies nothing, even if the member was changed in between, so a late retry cannot re-grant property access that someone else has since removed or revoke a member who was renewed. The same id with a different change, actor, account or action returns 409 and nothing is applied. A replayed `invite_member` answers with the saved invitation status instead (`{ok:true, duplicate:true, accountId, memberId, email, delivery:{channel, status}}`, see **Invitation delivery**) and never returns the invitation link again (only its hash is stored); send a new invitation for a fresh link. Requests without a `requestId` have no receipt.

## Invitation delivery

- **Manual (default).** Every invitation is a single-use private link shown once to the person who created it. This is the only mode while `BUSINESS_HUB_INVITE_DELIVERY` is unset, and it is always available (`deliver:'manual'`). Client administrators always use it.
- **Email (staff only).** With `BUSINESS_HUB_INVITE_DELIVERY=true`, staff can choose `deliver:'email'` when onboarding, inviting, resending or resetting. The browser asks for confirmation first, and that click is the human approval. The send goes through the approved-send core only (`b2b_invite` policy, `template+human_trigger`): it uses the owner-approved **Business hub invitation** wording, a HighLevel contact upserted from the saved member email and re-read to verify the location and email, and the contact's email do-not-disturb setting. Business managers can send for any account; sales staff can send only for accounts they own. The core refuses every other role, including crew and sales through `/api/messages`, before it reads any record. Email also needs `EGC_MESSAGING_ENABLED=true`, `EGC_MESSAGING_DRY_RUN=false`, the HighLevel key and location, and the owner's approval of the seeded draft wording. Otherwise the result is `not_sent`, `dry_run`, `not_configured` and so on, and the dialog shows the private link as the fallback.
- **One commit, then one send.** The new link hash, `member.invite` `{generation, channel, status:'sending', attemptId, sentBy, requestedAt, sends}` and the receipt are written in one preconditioned commit. The email then goes out in the same request, because the raw token exists only in memory. The outcome is saved against that exact generation and claim with a version-checked retry loop. A 4xx is `failed`; a 5xx, timeout or missing message id is `uncertain`. Nothing is retried automatically. The `message_sends` ledger key is the member generation, so each link is emailed at most once. The ledger, account, receipt and audit records keep only the redacted text (`[secure link]`) and the SHA-256 hash.
- **Resend and reset.** **Resend** (`resend_invite`) works for pending or expired invitations and for members whose seven-day sign-in has ended. **Reset sign-in** (`reset_sign_in`, which requires `confirm:true`) works for a signed-in member. Both issue a new generation, so every earlier link returns 401 and any current session ends. Nobody can renew their own access from a client session. Revoked people are restored only through the invite form. The last-active-administrator rule still applies to clients. `revoke_member` records `revokedAt` and `revokedBy`; revoking someone who is already revoked returns `{ok:true, unchanged:true}` and keeps the first record.
- **Limits.** Three caps apply per rolling 24 hours, all measured with the injected clock and all answered with 429 before anything is written or sent: at most three invitation emails per member (`member.invite.sends`), at most three to one mailbox across every account (the lowercase address with any `+tag` removed), and at most 20 per EGC staff user across every account. The mailbox and sender caps live in `business_operations/{sha256}` records (`kind:'invite_email_quota'`, hashed ids, no address stored) that join the invitation's preconditioned commit, so parallel sends cannot both pass. Only emails that were accepted or may have been delivered (`submitted`, `uncertain`) count: an attempt that certainly sent nothing (`not_sent`, `dry_run`, `suppressed`, `needs_contact`, `contact_mismatch`, `not_configured`, `unavailable`, `failed`) is taken back off all three. A private link is not limited.
- **Wording and names.** The owner cannot approve **Business hub invitation** wording that lacks `{{inviteLink}}`. If such a version is active anyway (approved before this rule), nothing is sent and the result is `not_sent` / `template_missing_invite_link` with the private link. Member names must be plain names (letters, spaces and `' ’ . , & ( ) -`; no digits, `/`, `:`, `@` or web-address shapes such as `example.com` or `www.`), because the first word greets the person in EGC's email. A name saved before this check is never emailed (409 until the person is invited again with the name corrected), and the recipient resolver gives the messaging core no name for it. The staff confirmation for **Email new link** shows the person's name as well as the address.
- **Status.** Team viewers (administrators and staff) see `inviteStatus` (pending, expired, active, sign_in_ended or revoked), `deliveryStatus`, `lastSentAt` and `expiresAt` for each member. Members invited before this release show `manual`, with the issue time taken as 48 hours before expiry. Other roles see none of these fields, and nobody sees attempt ids, provider ids or who sent the invitation.
- **Audit.** Every membership change writes a `business_audit` row with `details`: `memberId`, `role` (and `previousRole` when it changed), `generation`, `channel` (and `requestedChannel` when email was off), `previousStatus`, and for delivery rows the `status`, `reason` and whether the attempt was `released` from the caps. Sign-in and sign-out rows name the member and generation. Each member also keeps its last ten access changes in `accessHistory` (event, time, by, role, generation), so a later invitation does not erase who revoked them. Neither is shown to clients.
- **Sessions.** Redeeming an invitation clears the customer-portal project cookie, like logout, and ends the business session the browser held before (its member shows `sign_in_ended`), so a shared browser cannot keep an earlier company's access. Every redeem and logout deletes up to 20 expired `business_sessions` records, as a best effort. Each delete is preconditioned on the version that was read. The purge runs after the response through `waitUntil` (without it, the response waits at most two seconds), and each Firestore call has a five-second timeout, so it never holds up sign-in or sign-out.

## Data and safety

Server-only collections: `business_accounts`, `business_sessions`, `business_audit`, `business_operations` (idempotency receipts and invitation email caps). Direct browser SDK access remains denied by the deny-all rule at the end of `firestore.rules`, for every role including signed-in business staff; the emulator suite (`tests/firestore-emulator.test.mjs`) writes each of them through the real hub and checks that every read, list, create, update and delete is refused, and `tests/business-hub-hardening.test.mjs` fails if a rule ever grants any `business_*` collection. Invitations and sessions store SHA-256 hashes; raw invitation links are returned only to the authorized creator and are never written to account DTOs or analytics. Cookies are host-only and HttpOnly. State-changing API requests require exact same origin, JSON and a custom header. Account edits, audit and sharing mutations use Firestore preconditioned atomic commits.

A company session delegates only its exact authorized project with a `biz_...` collaborator identity. The individual project API rechecks current account status, member role/generation and canonical job grant on each request. The business session cannot become the homeowner owner session or access household collaborators, unrelated property memory, credits or membership balances. No signed customer agreement is altered.

A linked project (non-empty `businessAccountId`) never receives a homeowner owner-level portal link, which would bypass the company's member roles. The accepted-quote invitation records `suppressed` with reason `business_account_job` in the server-only `portal_invitations` ledger before any HighLevel call (the Hub shows "Business Hub project · no homeowner portal link"), and the job is re-read after the delivery claim so a link saved mid-send still releases the claim before HighLevel is messaged. **Copy customer portal** returns 409, and a homeowner link that predates the business link cannot create collaborator invitations (the portal hides **Copy invite** via `experience.invitesAvailable`). Unlinking clears the marker and restores homeowner delivery on the next check. Homeowner links shared before a project was linked keep working until staff revoke them with `/api/customer-portal-revoke`.

The Hub's approved customer messages (on-my-way, day-before and deposit reminders, estimate expiring, review request, follow-ups) never go to a linked project's job phone or email, which may be a tenant or an on-site contact: every customer-audience job policy refuses it first (`messaging_not_eligible`, reason `business_account_job`), before any link is minted, HighLevel contact is looked up or `message_sends` row is claimed. A later visit lineage-linked to a linked project's account root (`customerAccountOwnerJobId`, verified hop by hop as a portal link resolves it) is refused the same way, even for wording with no link, although the visit has no `businessAccountId` of its own; a lineage that needs review proves no root, so only the visit's own fields count there. The messaging cron counts linked projects under `skipped.business_account_job` and never attempts them; its scan cannot see a visit's lineage, so such a visit is refused once per Denver day at send and then counted under `skipped.business_account_job` for the rest of that day (the day's refusal is kept in `messaging_holds`). As a backstop, the `portalLink`/`payLink` providers refuse a job whose own or account root `businessAccountId` is set (`messaging_not_eligible` with reason `link_not_allowed_business_account`, so `/api/messages` still answers 409). Crew schedule notices for the job are unchanged. Invoices and overdue reminders are HighLevel's workflows (M5-SEND), not Hub messages. Routing B2B billing to the company's billing contact is future work.

A company member's quote approval saves `approvedByActorId` (the `biz_...` identity, including the member generation), `approvedByBusinessAccountId` and `approvedByBusinessMemberId` on the job's `customerApproval` and `estimate`, so AP sign-offs stay attributable after a member is renamed, re-roled or revoked. The ids are never returned to the portal. Homeowner and family approvals keep their earlier shape. A later portal approval, a staff **Record approval**, a material Hub estimate revision and a walkthrough signature all drop an earlier approver's ids from the new approval and estimate; a `superseded` approval keeps them as the record of who approved the earlier version. `customerApproval` is authoritative: trust these ids only when its `status` is `approved` and `source` is `customer_portal`.

Account storage limits: 100 properties, 300 requests, 100 linked projects, 30 members, 400 messages and a conservative 750KB JSON limit. Limit errors leave existing records unchanged. Archive/export tooling is a follow-on; do not promise unlimited retention. Owner account lists paginate at 50; sales lists explicitly report truncation above 50 rather than silently presenting a full list. Expired session retention/cleanup should be configured as operations volume grows.

**Retention.** Every `business_operations` record carries `expireAt`, a Firestore timestamp for a TTL policy on that collection:

- Request receipts (`create_account`, `invite_member`, `resend_invite`, `reset_sign_in`, `revoke_member`, `set_member_properties`, including a no-op's) expire 30 days after the request (`RECEIPT_DAYS` in `business-hub-core.js`). The page retries a lost response with the same id only while it stays open. Once Firestore has deleted a receipt, a request that reuses its id is treated as a new request.
- Invitation email cap records (`kind:'invite_email_quota'`) expire one rolling day after their last write. By then every send they counted has left the 24-hour window, so deleting them changes no limit.
- Nothing depends on the deletion. Firestore deletes expired documents some time after `expireAt` (typically within a day), and until then an expired receipt still answers its replay and an expired cap record counts nothing. `business_audit`, `hub_audit` and the account's own `accessHistory` never expire.
- Records written before this change have no `expireAt`, so the policy keeps them until they are backfilled. `node scripts/business-operations-ttl-backfill.mjs` (dry run by default, with `FIREBASE_SERVICE_ACCOUNT_JSON`) lists them; `--apply` writes only `expireAt` on each, the value the hub writes today (a receipt's `at` plus 30 days; a cap record one day after the later of its `updatedAt` and its newest send), fenced on the record's updateTime, so a record the hub rewrites during the run keeps its own value. A record whose time cannot be read is listed and never written. Dates already past are written as they are, so the policy then removes those records, which changes no answer. A rerun writes nothing. The report holds record ids and counts only.
- Owner step (once): enable the policy with `gcloud firestore fields ttls update expireAt --collection-group=business_operations --enable-ttl --project=egcw-1ec83`, then check it with `gcloud firestore fields ttls list --project=egcw-1ec83`. `firestore.indexes.json` declares the same policy (`fieldOverrides`: `ttl:true`, and `expireAt` is not indexed), so `firebase deploy --only firestore:indexes` applies it as well (that deploy also creates the file's composite indexes). Until the policy is on, these records are simply kept. Then run the backfill once (dry run, review, `--apply`).

## Boundaries of this release

- Requests and company messages are saved in this workspace. They do **not** automatically send email/SMS or enter HighLevel. The only email is a member invitation that staff explicitly choose to send, as described above. Staff must review the workspace; customer-facing text says so. Request-to-dispatch conversion is manual through existing tools.
- Job-level approval, payments, photo upload and rebooking use the existing project portal, not a second financial ledger. This release does not add bulk invoice payment or consolidated invoicing. CSV is a list of issued invoices, with formula-safe cells.
- The company's actual partner pricing is confirmed in each quote. There is no automatic percentage reprice of historical jobs. Original acquisition/commission attribution is preserved.
- No real companies, customers, invoices or payments are seeded by deployment. UI test fixtures are synthetic and are not committed as business data.
- Zoe's existing staff account/role must be verified in production. Do not grant employee-wide administrative access merely to enable the sales workspace.

## Extending the hub

- Server: add a `functions/_lib/business-hub-<name>.js` that exports any of `actions`, `exporters` and `decorate`. Register it in `functions/_lib/business-hub-modules.js` with one import line and one `MODULES` entry. Registered actions run only after the built-in CSRF, origin, JSON, size, session and active-account checks. Built-in action names cannot be replaced, and duplicate names across modules fail at load. Exports (`GET ?export=<name>`) need a session and an account. Every module response is served with `Cache-Control: no-store`, including redirects and fetched responses.
- Browser: add one deferred `/business-hub-<name>.js?v=<core version>` script line after the core script in `business-hub.html`, and register through `window.EGCBusinessHub` (`registerTab`, `registerRequestActions`, `registerMemberColumns`, `registerRenderHook`, `registerSubmitHook`, `api`, `exportUrl`, `showInvite`). `registerMemberColumns(fn, label, visible)` takes an optional `visible(data)` predicate like `registerTab`'s. A render hook `fn(content, data, tab)` runs after every account render; a submit hook `fn(form, payload, data)` for a built-in form kind (such as `invite`) can add fields to its payload or throw an Error to stop it with a message. `business-hub-team.js` (per-property access) and `business-hub-invites.js` (the Invitation column with Resend and Reset sign-in) are the reference modules.
- Reloads keep what was typed: the re-render after a save, a row action or Refresh puts back every other `form[data-form]` someone typed in (its values and its `data-id` request ID), the `<details>` panels left open and the caret of the field being typed in. The form just submitted is reset as before and its panel closes, and so is any copy of it that a reload (Refresh, a row action) drew while it was saving; a request ID that was saved is never put back. Controls are matched by name, type and, for checkboxes and radios, value; each value put back fires a bubbling `change` event, so a render hook's own listeners (such as the invitation's property access) follow it.
- Server helpers passed to actions and exports also include `canSeeProperty(ctx, propertyId)` and `scoped(ctx)` (the member's filtered account). `property(ctx, id)` answers 403 for a property outside the member's access. `save()` persists only the stored account object itself: the account the hub read (or built for a new company), changed in place, an account re-read with `store.read('business_accounts', id)` (for example to retry after a conflict), or a copy made with `copyStoredAccount(ctx.account)` from `business-hub-service.js`, the only public way to make one. Only the hub can mark an account as stored; no module exports that marker. A scoped view, a spread copy (`{...ctx.account}`, `{...scoped(ctx)}`) or a clone is refused with 503 and nothing is written, so a filtered account can never be saved over the full one and drop the properties it hides. The contents are checked as well as the object: when the hub reads an account, builds one for a new company or copies one with `copyStoredAccount`, it records the ids in `properties`, `requests`, `projects` (by `jobId`), `messages` and `members`. No action removes an entry (`unlink_project` sets `active:false`, `revoke_member` sets a status, and a full list refuses new entries rather than trimming old ones), so a `business_accounts` write that no longer holds every recorded id is refused with the same 503 and nothing is written. That covers copying a scoped view's contents into the stored account (`Object.assign(ctx.account, scoped(ctx))`, `ctx.account.properties = scoped(ctx).properties`) and filtering a `copyStoredAccount` copy. A module that needs to hide an entry must mark it, never remove it. The `store` helper applies the same rules with every store (so unit tests on an in-memory store and production agree): its `commit`, and `save()`'s extra writes, refuse any `business_accounts` write that is not a stored account under its own id, including a partial `patch`, or that lost a recorded entry. `commit` copies each write once and checks and commits only that copy (the account as one snapshot), so a getter cannot pass the check with the full account and then hand the store a filtered one; a write object with a getter is refused with 503. Exporters and decorators receive the filtered account, but registered actions receive the full account because they change it and call `save()`: an action that returns account rows (a list or summary) must filter them with `scoped(ctx)` or `canSeeProperty`.

## Rolling back past per-property access

Code from before per-property access (B2B-SCOPE) ignores `member.propertyIds`, so a rollback past it fails open: every member limited to selected properties would see every property of their company (requests, linked projects, balances, invoices and request messages), and delegated `/customer-portal` project access would no longer be checked per property. Rolling back past this hardening release alone is safe (older code ignores `expireAt`; the TTL policy keeps working on the records that have it). Before a rollback past B2B-SCOPE:

1. Export the limits (read-only, never writes): `FIREBASE_SERVICE_ACCOUNT_JSON='<service account JSON>' node scripts/business-members-scope-export.mjs --out b2b-member-scopes.json`. The file is created private (mode 0600) and names people (name and email), so keep it private. With `--out` the members are written only to that file and the terminal shows one summary line; if the file cannot be written privately, nothing about the members is printed and the script exits 1. It never contains invitation hashes, sessions or history. A partial scan fails instead of printing a shorter list.
2. For every member with `widensOnRollback: true` (an active or invited member, whatever the company's `accountStatus`), open the company in `/business-hub?staff=1` → **Team access** and choose **Revoke access** for the member. That ends their session and delegated project access on their next request. Only when the company confirms the person may see every property, choose **Edit property access** → **All properties, including ones added later** instead. A member of an inactive company is listed too, because reactivating the company would restore their session or invitation with every property. The hub refuses every change to an inactive company, so set its `status` to `active` in the Firestore console (the current code still enforces the limit), revoke the member, then set it back to `inactive`.
3. Run the export again. `widensOnRollback` must be `0` before you deploy the rollback.
4. While rolled back, do not invite or renew people who should be limited, and do not reactivate a company, because that code cannot limit their members.
5. After rolling forward again, restore each saved limit with **Edit property access**, or invite the person again with the property checkboxes, using the export.
