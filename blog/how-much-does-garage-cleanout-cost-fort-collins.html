Flat-Rate vs. Hourly Pricing: Which Is Better?

Most junk removal and garage cleanout companies charge one of two ways: hourly or flat-rate. Understanding the difference matters a lot when you're getting quotes.

Hourly pricing

Some companies charge $80–$140/hour with a two-person crew. On paper this sounds fair, but in practice it creates friction: the job takes longer than estimated, you get a surprise bill at the end, and you're watching the clock instead of pointing at things that need to go. A moderately full single-car garage can take 3–5 hours with sorting and hauling, which puts you at $240–$700 just in labor — before any dump fees.

Flat-rate pricing

A flat-rate quote means you know the number before anyone shows up. The price is locked regardless of whether the job takes two hours or six. This is almost always better for the customer because it eliminates the anxiety about how long things are taking. It also incentivizes the crew to work efficiently rather than drag out the clock.

What Drives the Cost of a Garage Cleanout Up

If a company quotes you more than our rates above, here's what usually explains it:

  • Volume of stuff. A garage that's floor-to-ceiling packed with boxes, furniture, and equipment requires more trips and more time at the dump. Our flat rates are calibrated to typical Fort Collins garages — if yours is unusually full (estate-level), we'll discuss a custom quote upfront.
  • Heavy or awkward items. A riding lawn mower, cast iron tub, or gun safe takes more time and sometimes special handling. We still haul these — it's just something to mention when you call.
  • Access difficulties. Garages that share a wall with a tight alley, have narrow doors, or require items to be carried up or down stairs take longer.
  • Dump fees. Larimer County dump fees vary by material weight. Some items — tires, mattresses, appliances — cost more to dispose of legally. We absorb all of this in our flat rate.
  • Hazardous materials. We can't legally haul certain items (more on this in our what we can and can't haul guide). If your garage has a lot of paint, motor oil, or propane tanks, that'll affect what we can take and what you'll need to handle separately.

The DIY Comparison: Renting a Truck and Doing It Yourself

Let's be honest about the DIY option. A lot of people consider renting a truck and hauling stuff themselves. Here's what that actually looks like:

  • Truck rental: A Home Depot or U-Haul pickup truck runs $80–$120 for a day, plus mileage.
  • Larimer County Landfill dump fees: Residential loads are charged by weight. A full load of household junk typically runs $120–$200 at the landfill off Timberline.
  • Multiple trips: Most garages don't fit in one truck load. Two trips = double the dump fees and rental time.
  • Your time: A full-day garage cleanout with two people is a serious physical undertaking. Realistically, 6–10 hours of labor between you and whoever you rope in to help.
  • Donation runs: If you want to donate, that's a separate trip to ReStore or Goodwill before your dump run — otherwise it all goes to the landfill.

When you add it up: $300–$500 in truck and dump fees, plus a full day of your time and physical labor. For most people over 40 (and honestly for most people period), paying $400–650 to have it handled completely is the better deal.

The Donation Receipt: Real Dollar Value

Here's something most people don't factor in: if you itemize deductions on your federal taxes, donated items have real monetary value. Furniture, working appliances, tools, and household goods donated to a 501(c)(3) organization like Habitat for Humanity ReStore can be deducted at fair market value.

A typical garage cleanout with us generates $100–$300 in deductible donations. We coordinate the drop-off at the Fort Collins ReStore and provide you an itemized receipt in your name. If you're in the 22% tax bracket, a $300 donation receipt saves you $66 in actual taxes — it's not everything, but it's real money and it takes zero effort on your part.
