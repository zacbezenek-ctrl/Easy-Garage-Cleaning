What Each Tier Actually Looks Like

Ranges are only useful if you can place your own garage in one, so here's what each tier looks like in real life.

Basic single-car clear-out ($400–650)

A one-car garage, or one bay of a two-car, that's cluttered but not buried: boxes along the walls, some furniture, yard tools, the usual accumulation. The crew sorts everything into keep, donate, recycle, and haul piles, loads the truck, makes the donation drop-off, and sweeps the floor before the keepers go back in. Most of these jobs are done in a morning.

Full two-car cleanout + donation runs ($650–1,200)

The classic Fort Collins job: a two-car garage that hasn't held a car in years. Floor-to-ceiling sections, furniture from two households ago, a wall of boxes nobody has opened since the move. This tier covers the full sort, multiple donation and recycling runs — to the Habitat for Humanity ReStore, ARC, metal recycling, and electronics recycling — plus the landfill load for what's genuinely done, and the sweep. Where the job lands inside the range comes down to volume and how much heavy material is in the mix.

Garage turnaround: shelving + organization ($1,200–2,200)

This is the tier where the garage doesn't just get empty — it gets fixed. Everything from the two-car cleanout, plus heavy-duty shelving installed on one or both walls, matched and labeled bins, a zone layout (tools, seasonal, sports), and everything off the floor so the cars actually fit. It's a cleanout and an organization project compressed into the same day, which is why we call it a turnaround. The garage organization page covers the system itself, and our garage transformation guide walks through the whole playbook step by step.

Whole-property reset incl. deep clean ($2,200–3,500)

The top of the range: a full turnaround plus a deep clean — walls wiped, cobwebs cleared, floor scrubbed rather than just swept — and the rest of the property folded in: the shed, the side yard, the basement corner that feeds the garage. These are the "getting the house ready to sell" and "twenty years in one weekend" jobs. They're also the most satisfying before-and-afters we do.

What Drives the Price Up or Down

Four factors decide where your garage lands in the $400–$3,500 range. When you call for a quote, these are exactly what we're listening for:

  • Volume. The biggest driver by far. A garage with clear floor and cluttered walls is a different job than a garage you can't walk into. Volume determines truck loads, and truck loads determine time, fuel, and disposal weight. This is why photos get you the most accurate quote — we're estimating cubic feet, not square feet.
  • Heavy and awkward items. A riding mower, a cast-iron tub, a gun safe, a stack of concrete pavers, a treadmill with a frame like a bank vault. We haul all of it, but heavy items change crew time and sometimes equipment, so they matter to the number. Mention them on the call and the quote will already account for them.
  • Hazards and special-handling items. Household hazardous waste — paint, motor oil, solvents, propane — legally can't ride with a general load, so garages with a serious chemical corner involve staging that material for you to take to Larimer County's household hazardous waste program. Other items, like tires and mattresses, carry their own disposal handling. None of this is a dealbreaker; it's just part of an honest quote. Our guide to what you can and can't throw away covers the full list.
  • Access. An alley-loaded Old Town garage with a narrow door is more carry time than a wide-open driveway off the Harmony corridor. Stairs, long carries, tight gates, and where the truck can park all factor in. It rarely moves a quote dramatically, but it's part of why we ask.

Notice what's not on the list: how embarrassed you are about the garage. We've seen everything, and the packed ones are the jobs we exist for. There's no judgment surcharge.

Flat-Rate vs. Hourly: Why the Pricing Model Matters

Cleanout companies generally price one of two ways, and the difference matters more than the headline number.

How hourly pricing plays out

Hourly sounds fair on paper: pay for the time it takes. In practice, you're signing up for three problems. First, the meter runs on things you can't see or control — traffic to the landfill, a slow sort, a second trip. Second, disposal fees usually get added on top after the fact, so the "rate" you compared against was never the real price. Third, the incentives point the wrong way: the slower the crew works, the more they make. Nearly every horror story you'll read in junk removal reviews follows the same arc — the job "took longer than expected," fees appeared at the end, and the final bill landed far above the number that was discussed. The structure produces the story.

How flat-rate pricing plays out

A flat rate means the number is agreed on before anyone lifts anything, and it doesn't move whether the job takes three hours or seven. That requires us to actually scope the job first — by phone, by photos, or with a quick walkthrough — which is work, and it's why some companies don't do it. But it puts the incentives where they belong: we're motivated to work efficiently, and you're free to spend the day pointing at things instead of watching a clock. If the crew hits something unexpected, that's our problem, not your bill.

At Easy Garage Cleaning, we quote flat-rate only, and the quote is locked before work starts. The pricing page explains the whole approach.

What's Always Included in the Flat Rate

Every garage cleanout we do in Fort Collins includes all of the following — at every tier, with no add-on menu:

  • All labor and lifting. A full crew, start to finish. You point; we carry. You never touch the heavy end of anything.
  • All hauling. Truck, fuel, loading, and as many runs as the job takes.
  • The donation run, with receipts. Usable furniture, appliances, tools, and household goods go to the Habitat for Humanity ReStore or ARC, dropped off in your name, with the itemized receipt back in your hand for tax season.
  • Recycling. Scrap metal and electronics are separated and recycled as a standard part of the sort — including e-waste, which Colorado restricts from landfills anyway. The full routing is in our guide to where your stuff actually goes after a cleanout.
  • Every disposal fee. The Larimer County landfill charges by weight, and whatever our loads cost to dispose of legally is our cost, inside your flat rate — never a surprise line item.
  • The sweep. Every cleanout ends with a fully swept floor. A deep clean (walls, cobwebs, scrubbed floor) is available as part of the upper tiers.

Every tier leaves the garage swept out. Want the full wipe-down — cobwebs, dust, walls, stain treatment? Our standalone garage cleaning service runs $150–$300 and books as an add-on or on its own.

If you're comparing quotes from multiple companies, this checklist is the comparison: ask each one which of these is included and which shows up later as a fee. The cheapest headline number is frequently the most expensive invoice.

Day-Of Decision Factors: How the Quote Stays Locked

People sometimes worry the flat rate is a teaser — that the real number shows up once the truck is in the driveway. So here's exactly how day-of decisions work on our jobs:

  • Your keep-or-go calls are built in. The morning walkthrough sets the plan, and normal day-of judgment calls — keeping the dresser you thought you'd toss, tossing the bikes you thought you'd keep — don't move the price. The quote was for the garage, not an itemized manifest.
  • Surprises get flagged, not billed. If we open the attic hatch and find a second garage's worth of storage that wasn't in the photos, we stop and talk before touching it. You approve a revised number or we stick to the original scope. Either way, nothing is hauled and then invoiced.
  • Add-ons are quoted before they happen. The most common day-of request is upgrading mid-job: "While you're here — could you do shelving on that wall too?" or "What would the deep clean run?" The answer is always a specific number, agreed on before we continue. Many of our turnaround jobs started as cleanouts at 8 a.m.
  • Finishing early doesn't raise the rate, and running long doesn't either. That's the whole point of flat-rate. The number you approved is the number you pay.

The DIY Comparison, Honestly

Could you do this yourself? Of course — people do it every spring. But price the whole project before you decide. A DIY garage cleanout means renting a truck or trailer (charged by the day, plus mileage), paying the Larimer County landfill's by-weight rate on every load you bring in, making the donation drop-offs as separate trips on the donation centers' schedules, figuring out which recycler takes the metal and the old TV, and supplying the labor — realistically a full weekend or two of heavy lifting for you and whoever you can talk into helping.

When people add up the rentals, the per-load fees, the fuel, and the weekends, the cash cost alone often lands within sight of the bottom of our range — before counting the labor, the sore back, and the donation receipt that DIY trips rarely produce because everything ends up going to the landfill in frustration by trip three. For a single carload of clutter, DIY wins. For a real garage cleanout, hiring it out is usually the cheaper option once your time is worth anything at all. Our DIY vs. professional comparison runs the full breakdown.

The Donation Receipt: Money Most People Leave on the Table

One more number belongs in the cost math: the donation receipt. Usable goods from your cleanout go to the ReStore or ARC, and the itemized receipt comes back in your name. If you itemize deductions on your federal return, donated goods can generally be claimed at fair market value — which means a garage full of decent furniture and working tools has real tax value that a dumpster gives you none of. We're not tax advisors, so the specifics belong to your CPA, but our guide to donation tax deductions explains how the receipt works.
