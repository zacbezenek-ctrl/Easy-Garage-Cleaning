Where Your Stuff Actually Goes After a Fort Collins Garage Cleanout

Quick summary

Keep, donate, recycle, haul: usable goods go to the Habitat for Humanity ReStore and ARC (receipt back to you), metal and electronics get recycled, and only true end-of-life items go to the Larimer County landfill. Sorting, donation runs, and disposal fees are all inside the flat-rate price.

The first question everyone asks about a garage cleanout is what it costs. The second — usually asked a little more quietly — is "so… where does all of this actually go?" It's a good question, and the answer matters. Nobody loves the mental image of a whole garage's worth of furniture, tools, and kids' bikes getting tipped into a hole in the ground. The good news: on a well-run Fort Collins garage cleanout, the landfill is the last resort, not the plan.

At Easy Garage Cleaning, sorting is built into every job we do. Before anything goes on the truck, it goes into one of four piles — keep, donate, recycle, haul — and each pile has a real destination: the Habitat for Humanity ReStore, ARC, Northern Colorado scrap and electronics recyclers, and only then the Larimer County landfill for the things nothing else will take. This guide walks through exactly how that works, category by category, so you know where your stuff ends up before you ever book.

The Four-Pile System: Keep, Donate, Recycle, Haul

Every garage cleanout we run in Fort Collins — from Old Town bungalow garages to three-car garages off the Harmony corridor — uses the same sorting system. It's deliberately simple, because when you're staring down fifteen years of accumulation, simple is what actually gets finished.

Pile 1: Keep

This is the only pile you have to build, and you build it by pointing. Anything staying gets staged in one spot — usually the driveway or a single corner — so the garage can be completely emptied and swept before the keepers go back in. If you've booked a full garage turnaround, the keep pile is also where the zone and shelving plan starts. Most people are surprised by how small this pile turns out to be once everything is sitting in daylight instead of stacked in the dark.

Pile 2: Donate

Anything with usable life left: furniture that still works as furniture, appliances that run, hand and power tools, sporting goods, housewares, books, and the endless stream of outgrown kids' gear. This pile rides to the Habitat for Humanity ReStore or ARC — donation runs are part of the job, not an add-on — and the itemized receipt comes back to you for tax season.

Pile 3: Recycle

Scrap metal, electronics, cardboard, and appliances that no longer run. None of this needs to be landfilled, and most of it shouldn't be. Metal is endlessly recyclable, and Colorado restricts most electronics from landfill disposal anyway, so this pile is about doing it right rather than doing it fast.

Pile 4: Haul

What's left at the end: the broken, the moldy, the water-damaged, the genuinely done. This is the only pile that goes to the Larimer County landfill, and the disposal fees for it are already inside your flat-rate price. No surprise line items afterward.

That's the whole division of labor: you make the keep decisions, we handle every destination after that. You never load a truck, drive a load, or stand in a drop-off line.

Donation Runs: ReStore, ARC, and a Receipt With Your Name on It

The donate pile is usually the best part of a cleanout — and it's the part a dumpster can never give you.

The Habitat for Humanity ReStore accepts furniture, working appliances, tools, and building materials, and the proceeds support Habitat's affordable-housing work here in Northern Colorado. ARC takes clothing, housewares, toys, books, and the general household goods every garage accumulates. Between the two, most usable items in a typical Fort Collins garage have a local landing spot where they'll actually get used again.

We load it, we drive it, we drop it off in your name, and the itemized receipt comes back to you. If you itemize deductions on your federal return, donated goods can generally be claimed at fair market value. We're not tax advisors and won't pretend to be — but we will make sure you walk away holding the paperwork. Our guide to donation tax deductions covers how that works, and our Fort Collins ReStore guide covers what they do and don't accept.

One honest caveat: donation centers can and do decline items — stained mattresses, ripped upholstery, particle-board furniture past its prime. When that happens, we don't bring it back to your driveway. It simply moves to the recycle or haul pile and the job carries on. You never have to manage any of it.

Metal: The Most Recyclable Thing in Your Garage

Garages are full of metal — old wire shelving, bike frames the kids outgrew, rusted patio furniture, a dead grill, the water heater the plumber swapped out and left behind, coffee cans of mixed hardware. All of it can skip the landfill.

Steel and aluminum are among the most recyclable materials there are; they can be melted down and reused essentially without limit. Northern Colorado has established scrap recyclers, and the metal loads from our cleanouts ride there instead of to the dump. Mowers, snowblowers, and other small-engine equipment can be scrapped too once fluids are drained — we handle that routing so you don't have to think about it.

The practical takeaway for you is simple: don't pre-sort the metal, don't drag it to the curb, and definitely don't pay to landfill it. It's handled as part of the job, and it's a big part of why the haul pile at the end of our cleanouts is smaller than people expect.

E-Waste: Handled Responsibly on Every Job

Old electronics are the category people feel guiltiest about — and the one with actual rules attached. Colorado restricts most electronics from landfill disposal: TVs, monitors, computers, laptops, printers, and the like are supposed to be recycled, not dumped.

On our jobs, that's standard rather than an add-on. Electronics that come out of your garage get separated on the spot and routed to electronics recycling instead of being mixed into the haul pile. You don't need to ask for it, flag the items, or pay a separate "eco fee" at the end — responsible handling is just how every job runs, and the cost is already inside the flat rate.

If your garage is hiding a CRT television from the Clinton administration, you're in good company; bring it out and it gets recycled like everything else. Old appliances follow a similar logic — working units get donated, dead ones get recycled for their metal — and we wrote up the details in what to do with old appliances in Fort Collins.

What Genuinely Has to Go to the Larimer County Landfill

We're not going to pretend everything is salvageable. Some portion of almost every garage cleanout is genuinely at end of life: water-damaged boxes, moldy carpet remnants, broken particle-board furniture, cracked storage totes, dried-up sealant tubes, and the foam, fabric, and fragments that no donation center can accept and no recycler can use.

That material goes to the Larimer County landfill, properly and legally — and the disposal fees for it are included in your flat-rate quote, not billed back to you afterward as line items.

Two things keep this pile honest. First, it gets built last: nothing lands in it until the donate and recycle piles have had their shot. Second, you can see it. The sorting happens in your driveway, in daylight, and you're welcome to look at exactly what's headed to the landfill and pull anything back out. Companies that throw everything into one truck to "sort it later" are asking you to take that on faith. The driveway sort means you don't have to.

The Stuff We Can't Take (and Where It Goes Instead)

Household hazardous waste is the one category that doesn't ride on our truck: paint and stains, motor oil, antifreeze, gasoline, solvents, pesticides, pool chemicals, and propane tanks. Haulers aren't allowed to transport that mixed in with general loads, and none of it belongs in a landfill anyway.

It's not a dead end, though. Larimer County runs a household hazardous waste program for residents, and most of what's in your garage's "chemical corner" can go there. During a cleanout we'll gather it, stage it neatly in one spot, and tell you exactly what you're looking at — so the one errand left on your plate is a single trip. Our guide to what you can and can't throw away in Fort Collins goes deeper on this category.

Why This Beats a Dumpster in the Driveway

The do-it-yourself alternative is renting a roll-off dumpster, and it's worth being clear-eyed about what that actually buys you:

  • One destination for everything. A dumpster has no donate pile, no recycle run, and no receipt. The couch that could have furnished someone's first apartment goes to the landfill right alongside the moldy carpet.
  • You're the labor. Every box, bench, and bike gets carried by you — usually across a weekend, often across several.
  • It sits there. For days, sometimes a week or more, in front of your house. HOAs in the newer neighborhoods along the Harmony corridor tend to have opinions about that. So does hail season, if your car gets displaced to the driveway while the dumpster takes its spot.
  • The price isn't always the price. Weight overages and prohibited-item fees are standard fine print in roll-off rental agreements, and you don't find out until the bill.

A crew-run cleanout inverts all of it: the sorting happens in hours, the donations and recycling actually get donated and recycled, the hauling and junk removal are simply included in the job, and by dinner your driveway is just a driveway again. We wrote a full side-by-side in junk removal vs. dumpster rental in Fort Collins if you're weighing the two.

FAQ: Where Your Stuff Goes

Do you donate items from Fort Collins garage cleanouts?

Yes. Donation runs are standard on every garage cleanout, not an upsell. Furniture, working appliances, tools, and household goods in usable shape go to the Habitat for Humanity ReStore or ARC, and the itemized donation receipt comes back to you.

Do I get a donation receipt for tax purposes?

Yes. We drop off donations in your name and hand you the itemized receipt. If you itemize deductions on your federal return, donated goods can generally be claimed at fair market value — your tax professional can tell you exactly what that's worth in your situation.

What happens to old TVs, computers, and other electronics?

Colorado restricts most electronics from landfill disposal, so TVs, monitors, computers, and printers are separated on the spot and routed to electronics recycling. Responsible e-waste handling is standard on every job — you don't need to request it or pay a separate fee.

What can't you take in a garage cleanout?

Household hazardous waste: paint, motor oil, antifreeze, gasoline, solvents, pesticides, pool chemicals, and propane tanks. Larimer County runs a household hazardous waste program for residents, so we stage those items neatly in one spot, tell you exactly what you're looking at, and point you to the right drop-off.

Is disposal included in the price, or billed separately?

Included. Garage cleanouts in Fort Collins run $400–$3,500 flat-rate depending on volume, condition, and how far the turnaround goes — and that number covers labor, hauling, donation runs, recycling, and every disposal fee. The price quoted before work starts is the price you pay.
