What a Garage Turnaround Actually Is

A garage cleanout gets the stuff out. A garage turnaround gets the garage back. The difference matters, because an empty-but-grimy garage with nowhere to put your keepers tends to refill in about a season.

A full turnaround stacks five jobs into one visit:

  • The cleanout. Everything comes out of the garage and into staged zones on the driveway — every box, every broken fan, the tangle of extension cords, all of it.
  • The sort. Keep, donate, haul. You make the calls; we do the carrying.
  • The donation run. Usable furniture, tools, sporting goods, and household items go to local donation partners like the Habitat for Humanity ReStore or ARC, and the receipt comes back to you.
  • The haul. The genuinely done-for stuff leaves on our truck and gets disposed of properly. Hauling and disposal are included in the job — there's no separate junk-removal bill bolted on at the end.
  • The reset. Sweep and deep garage cleaning, shelving installed on one or both walls, keepers placed back in by zone, and a final walkthrough before we pull away.

Do all five and you get a genuine garage transformation — not just a tidier version of the same problem. It's the difference between "we got rid of some stuff" and "we park in the garage again." And if you only need part of that list, that's fine too: the quote matches the scope, nothing more. If shelving and layout are the part you care most about, our garage organization page covers that side of the job in detail.

What You Do Beforehand (Honestly, Almost Nothing)

This is the part people don't believe until the morning of. Your entire prep list:

  • Get the cars out of the garage and driveway. We use the driveway as the sorting floor, so it needs to be clear when we arrive.
  • Flag the obvious keepers. Walk the garage for ten minutes the night before and point out — or stick a sticky note on — anything you already know is staying. The good toolbox. The camping bins. The kids' bikes.
  • Be reachable. You can sort shoulder-to-shoulder with us, check in once an hour from the kitchen, or answer the occasional photo text from work. Any of the three works.

That's it. Don't pre-sort. Don't buy bins. Don't spend the weekend before "getting it ready for the crew" — that's paying for a service and then performing the service yourself. We wrote a whole piece on how to prepare for a garage cleanout, and the honest summary is: pull the cars out and put the coffee on.

The One-Day Garage Turnaround, Hour by Hour

Every garage is a little different, but here's the rhythm of a typical packed two-car garage in Fort Collins — the kind where the door still closes, but nothing with wheels has parked inside in years.

8:00 a.m. — Walkthrough and game plan

Your flat-rate quote was locked in before we ever scheduled the day — usually from photos you texted over — so the morning walkthrough is about logistics, not money. We confirm where the keep zone lives, which wall is getting shelving, what's fragile, what's heavy, and whether anything needs a dolly and two sets of hands. Ten minutes, and then the door goes up for good.

8:15 a.m. — Everything comes out

The fastest way to sort a garage is to empty it. We carry everything into daylight and stage it across the driveway in loose categories: tools with tools, boxes with boxes, a mystery pile for the things nobody can identify yet. Garages hide volume; driveways reveal it. This is the stage where homeowners stand in the doorway and say some version of "I had no idea there was this much in there." There was. There always is.

9:30 a.m. — Three zones: keep, donate, haul

Now the day's only real decisions get made, and the system keeps them fast. Three clearly separated zones: keep along the fence, donate near the truck, haul on the curb side. We walk you through the gray-area items — is this humidifier actually going back inside, or has it been "going back inside" for three years? — and anything you're stuck on goes into a maybe pile instead of stalling the line. Most families get through the whole sort in about an hour, because they're pointing, not lifting.

11:00 a.m. — The donation run leaves

Usable furniture, tools, working small appliances, sporting goods, and household items get loaded separately and driven to local donation spots — the Habitat for Humanity ReStore and ARC are our usual stops, depending on what you've got and what they're accepting that week. The donation receipt comes back in your name. The haul load goes out right behind it: the broken, the rusted, the water-stained, the truly finished. That hauling and disposal is part of your flat rate — included in the job, never a surprise add-on.

12:30 p.m. — The empty garage gets cleaned

This is the moment the job turns from removal into turnaround. With the slab bare for the first time in years, we sweep the full footprint, knock down the cobwebs, and clear out the leaves and cottonwood fluff that drift into every Front Range garage over a summer. On deep-clean jobs we scrub and spot-treat the floor as far as effort will take it — and we'll tell you honestly which old oil stains are permanent residents rather than pretending otherwise.

1:30 p.m. — Shelving goes up

If your turnaround includes shelving — and for a two-car garage it usually should — this is when it gets anchored along one or both walls. Sturdy shelving changes the math of a garage: the bins and boxes that were living where your car should be move up onto the walls instead. We plan it around what you kept — holiday bins and camping gear up high, weekly-use items at reach height, heavy stuff down low. There's more on how we think about layout on our garage organization service page.

3:00 p.m. — Keepers go home, by zone

Everything in the keep zone comes back inside with some logic to it: a yard-and-garden zone near the side door, sports gear and bikes near the overhead door, the rarely-touched bins overhead or up top. The goal isn't a magazine photo. The goal is that six months from now you can find the tire inflator without moving eleven boxes to get to it.

4:30 p.m. — Final walkthrough, then the car rolls in

We walk the garage and driveway together. You confirm every keep item is present and placed, the donate and haul loads were exactly what you approved, and the floor and shelving look the way you wanted. Then comes the part people take phone videos of: backing the car into a garage it hasn't seen in years. "Parked inside by dinner" isn't a slogan — it's the spec the whole day is built around.

How We Protect the Things You're Keeping

The number-one worry we hear before a turnaround isn't price — it's "what if something I wanted ends up on the truck?" Fair worry. Here's the system that prevents it:

  • Nothing leaves without sign-off. The truck doesn't get loaded until you've looked over the donate and haul zones and given the go-ahead. You approve piles, not promises.
  • The maybe pile is sacred. Unsure items never default to the truck. They sit in their own spot until you've decided — even if that decision comes at the end of the day.
  • Keepers get handled like keepers. The keep zone is staged away from the loading path, fragile items get boxed or padded, and nothing keep-tagged gets stacked under something heavy.
  • Personal items get surfaced, not sorted. Photo albums, file boxes, paperwork, anything that looks sentimental or important — we bring it to you rather than making a judgment call on your behalf.
  • The final walkthrough is a checkpoint, not a courtesy. Before the truck leaves the curb, you've seen the finished garage and the loads. If anything needs to come back off, it comes back off.

The one rule of the day: nothing leaves the property until you've approved it. You can be hands-on for every box or hands-off until the walkthrough — the rule holds either way.

Why One Day Beats a Month of Weekends

The DIY version of this project fails for a predictable reason, and it isn't laziness — it's decision fatigue. Every box in a garage is a small decision, and many of them are emotional ones. Do it alone and you get through a corner, hit a box of the kids' school art, and the project quietly dies until next month. Meanwhile the garage keeps absorbing new stuff, so you're bailing a boat that's still taking on water.

A one-day garage turnaround works because everything happens inside the same window: the muscle, the truck, the sorting system, the donation run, and a place for every single item to actually go — same day. There's no half-sorted pile sitting in the driveway for a week, no third trip you keep meaning to make, and no chance for the garage to refill mid-project, because the project doesn't last long enough.

Front Range weather is the other quiet argument for compressing the job. A DIY cleanout spreads across whatever weekends happen to stay dry, and a surprise hailstorm or early cold snap can strand half your garage on the driveway. A turnaround happens inside one weather window, with a crew that's watching the sky and a truck that's already on site.

One-Day Garage Turnaround FAQs

Can a packed two-car garage really be done in one day?

Almost always, yes. A small crew working from a plan moves faster than most families expect — the sorting system does the heavy lifting, and we are not stopping to reminisce over every box. Very large jobs, extreme volume, or whole-property resets sometimes run into a second day, and we tell you that before we start, not after.

What do I need to do before the crew arrives?

Almost nothing. Move your cars out of the garage and driveway, point out anything you already know you want to keep, and make sure someone can answer the occasional question in person or by phone. You do not need to pre-sort, buy bins, or empty a single shelf — that is what you are hiring us for.

How do you make sure nothing I want to keep gets hauled away?

Nothing leaves the property until you have seen it in the donate or haul zone and signed off. You can sort with us, check in once an hour, or just review the staged piles before the truck is loaded — and we do a final walkthrough with you before we drive away. If you are on the fence about something, it goes in a maybe pile, not on the truck.

Every job is flat-rate, quoted up front, and the price is locked before we start.

Is junk removal included, or is that a separate charge?

It is included in the job. Hauling, disposal, and the donation run are all built into your flat-rate quote — one number covers the whole turnaround. You will never get a surprise line item for the truck load at the end of the day.
